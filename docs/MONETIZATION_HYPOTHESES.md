# Monetization hypotheses

> Status: product hypothesis only. This document does not authorize or implement purchases, subscriptions, entitlements, paywalls, currencies, energy, or account gating.

Detour's core promise is that when someone has a little time and does not know what to do, they can immediately go outside and notice a city differently.

The long-term value is not "many scavenger-hunt tasks." It is a point of view: **Detour knows how to make me notice things I normally overlook.**

Monetization should add distinct, curated perspectives without weakening that default experience.

## Core experience stays free

The normal Detour experience should remain free:

- core journeys
- basic history
- ticket
- photos
- sharing
- basic route experience

Avoid monetizing:

- journey count
- history retention
- photo storage
- better routing
- skip functionality
- basic safety or accessibility

Do not create artificial scarcity around the core loop. A person who opens Detour because they have ten spare minutes should not first have to calculate whether the moment is "worth" spending a life, ticket, energy unit, or daily allowance.

## Preferred initial model

If Detour eventually demonstrates retention and a willingness to pay, prefer **small one-time purchases for clearly different curated experiences** before forcing an early subscription.

Taiwan pricing hypotheses for testing:

| Hypothesis | Test price |
| --- | ---: |
| Small themed Detour | NT$30–40 |
| Richer Date / Photography Detour | NT$50–60 |
| Three-pack bundle | NT$90–120 |
| Season / city collection | NT$120–180 |

These are hypotheses, not final recommendations. No price should ship before the underlying experience proves that it feels meaningfully different from core.

## Candidate experience packs

### Night Detour

A visual observation experience built around the city after dark:

- artificial light
- reflections
- shadow boundaries
- illuminated signs
- glowing surfaces
- color contrast
- repeated light patterns

It must use the same safety rules and the same Journey engine. It should never reward entering hidden or dark places, approaching private property, following people or animals, or interacting with infrastructure.

The value is not "nighttime missions." It is learning to notice how familiar streets become visually different after dark.

### Date Detour

A shared observation experience, not a relationship quiz and not a cheesy romance flow.

Prompts should make two people look at the same environment and compare what they notice. Examples of product directions:

- each person quietly chooses which of two details they noticed first
- find two versions of the same pattern
- point out something the other person probably walked past
- compare which color, shape, repair, sign, or small urban detail stands out

The Journey engine, routing, ticket, camera, and memory system remain the same. The content perspective changes.

### Rain Detour

A rainy-city observation lens:

- reflections
- water traces
- shelter edges
- changed textures
- dripping surfaces
- puddle geometry
- materials that look different when wet

Safety is more important than novelty. A Rain Detour must not encourage stepping into roads, flooded areas, slippery infrastructure, or unsafe shelter.

### Photography Detour

Composition-oriented observation without becoming a camera-filter app.

Possible observation concepts:

- natural frames
- repeated patterns
- layers
- color blocks
- depth
- contrast
- foreground/background relationships
- negative space

The value is learning to see compositions in ordinary streets. Detour should not add editing presets or make photo production the mandatory proof of completion.

### City Packs

The strongest long-term direction may be culturally specific observation packs.

Example:

**Taipei — Alley Details**

Possible content themes:

- iron window grilles
- arcades / covered walkways
- tile facades
- utility boxes
- old signage
- rooftop additions
- scooter-shaped urban space
- patched architecture

A City Pack should not become a traditional sightseeing guide.

The value proposition is:

> Not "what exists in this city", but "how to look at this city."

A strong city pack can contain ordinary things that locals pass every day. Specificity should sharpen observation, not turn the product into a checklist of attractions.

## Creator Packs

A future Detour could let photographers, architects, urban observers, illustrators, designers, or local creators design observation experiences.

Conceptually, Detour provides:

- routing engine
- journey engine
- ticket / memory system
- camera support
- distribution

Creators provide:

- prompts
- observation perspective
- theme
- curation
- voice

Potential revenue sharing can be explored later.

Do **not** implement a marketplace now. Creator tooling, moderation, discovery, payouts, licensing, refunds, quality control, and content safety are all separate product problems that should only exist after curated first-party Experiences demonstrate value.

## Anti-patterns

Detour should avoid monetization based on:

- removing ads
- journey energy
- daily limits
- unlimited-play upgrades
- XP boosters
- coins
- streak protection
- better paid routing
- larger history limits
- paid photo retention
- AI-generated mission subscriptions without demonstrated value

Why:

These either damage the spontaneous-use loop or charge for something a user reasonably expects to be part of the basic Detour experience.

In particular:

- **Energy / daily limits** make "I have ten minutes right now" collide with an artificial gate.
- **Unlimited-play upgrades** imply the normal product intentionally prevents use.
- **Better paid routing** creates the wrong safety and trust boundary.
- **History/photo limits** monetize memories after the user already created them.
- **XP, coins, streaks, boosters** pull attention toward an economy instead of the city.
- **AI mission subscriptions** sell implementation technology rather than demonstrated user value.

## Immediate-start rule

Experience architecture must not turn the normal home screen into a configuration funnel.

The default primary action stays equivalent to:

> 繞一下？

A normal journey must not require choosing:

- mode
- duration
- mood
- party size
- theme

Themed Experiences can eventually be optional secondary choices. They should never become a mandatory question before every core Journey.

## Validation before monetization

Before implementing payment, a candidate paid Experience should demonstrate:

1. People can describe how it feels meaningfully different from core.
2. The difference comes from a coherent observation perspective, not cosmetic relabeling.
3. People want to replay or share it.
4. It remains safe and simple.
5. It can be maintained as content/configuration rather than a parallel game engine.

Only after those conditions are met should pricing, store presentation, entitlement logic, refunds, restore-purchase behavior, or revenue sharing become implementation work.
